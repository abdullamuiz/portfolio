Muiz here,

Digital Visibility Strategist & Content Creator

15+ years in Maldivian tourism marketing and behind the camera, helping hotels, guesthouses and local brands get seen by the right people.

I’m Abdulla Muiz Fuad, a Maldivian marketer, photographer and lecturer. Since 2011 I’ve worked on one question: how does a business in the Maldives get seen by the right people, and turn that attention into bookings and sales?

My career began at Highrise, working on the Maldives Travel Awards. In 2014, while studying for my bachelor’s and master’s in Kuala Lumpur, I joined the twice-yearly MATTA Fair travel shows as a junior sales host. Within a few shows I was leading the sales team for Maldives packages. It became my testing ground for making Maldivian hotels and guesthouses more attractive to buyers. Before each show I spent two to three months structuring packages, grouping them by type of traveller and designing a sales conversation that guided each visitor through a few simple choices. People left with a package that felt made for them, because in a way they had built it themselves.

Back home I was creative director at Blazon, producing inflight magazines and the Maldives Business Awards. I wrote for Maldives Ports Limited, then ran marketing at Reethi Beach Resort, where I learned how far one well-timed story can carry a quiet season. Later I led the SME HUB at Business Centre Corporation, helping 23 small businesses find their feet.

Through all of it I’ve stayed behind the camera. I shoot, direct and edit my own work, so the strategy and the content come from the same hands.

Visibility followed me into research too. My first paper, in 2017, looked at how Maldivian guesthouses could become more visible online. The second, in 2021, mapped the realities of running a guesthouse here and has been cited more than 36 times. Today I teach marketing, branding, e-marketing and international marketing at MNU Business School, and the classroom keeps me honest about what actually works.

I judge marketing by what it brings back: bookings, sales and returning customers, not views and likes. I believe a ten-room guesthouse deserves the same thinking a resort chain buys from an agency.

    Approach

    What fifteen years in the industry taught me.

    Most properties hire one person for strategy, another for content and an agency for reporting. Some skip reporting altogether, so ad spend goes out with no real way to see what it brings back.

    I work in systems: every expense is tracked, measured and accountable for its impact.

    18 to 35%

    OTAs made booking easier and marketing harder.

    Tour operators once briefed guests on the destination and handled problems as part of the sale. Online travel agencies replaced that with a Book now button, and properties pay 18 to 35% of every room night for it.

    Big chains hire agencies to build direct channels. A guesthouse with 10 rooms or fewer can do the same, with a little consistent extra work that pays back for years.

    10K ≠ 10K

    More posts don’t mean more customers.

    In food and beverage, the competition is fierce and the pressure to post never stops. But 10,000 followers doesn’t mean 10,000 customers in a month, or even in three.

    The Maldives market is finite. Repeat customers are what keep sales from bottoming out, so that’s who the content should serve.
