I teach the way I learned marketing: by doing it first. Since 2011 I’ve worked on one question, how a business in the Maldives gets seen by the right people and turns that attention into bookings and sales. Today I bring that question into the classroom.

My career began at Highrise, on the Maldives Travel Awards. While studying for my bachelor’s and master’s in Kuala Lumpur, I joined the MATTA Fair travel shows as a junior sales host and went on to lead the sales team for Maldives packages. Back home I worked at Blazon and Maldives Ports Limited, ran marketing at Reethi Beach Resort, and led the SME HUB at Business Centre Corporation, helping 23 small businesses find their feet.

I started teaching part-time at Mianz College in 2022 and joined MNU Business School in 2024. I’ve taught nine modules there since, from Introduction to Marketing to International Marketing, Electronic Marketing and Brand Management, and coordinated MKT101.

My students tell me what works. They asked for more practical, hands-on learning, and I’ve built three web apps that let them work on real cases and audit real businesses. [TODO: confirm when each app was built and which feedback prompted it]

My research grew the same way, from a 2017 paper on how guesthouses become visible online to the question I want to study next: how small tourism businesses get found when an AI does the searching.

I believe students learn marketing best when they work on real businesses, with real numbers.

Teaching philosophy

How I teach.

Graduates with employability skills.

I came from industry. In marketing management roles I sat on the other side of the desk, interviewing graduates. The knowledge was there, but when a real question came there was no reference point, and minds went blank. So I teach students to meet that manager, and surprise them.

Theory and application are worth more to an employer than academia or experience alone.

A moment of pride

Zero cancelled classes.

In Term 1 2025 our new dean challenged the department to get through a whole term without cancelling a class. I took it on and kept going: by my own record, I haven’t lost a class in 2025 or 2026. When a public holiday fell on a class day, I taught that class in advance. I also cover for colleagues when emergencies come up, and together we built a simple cover system in the department, so a class goes ahead whenever a free room and time slot allow.

In Term 2 2025 I taught 18 contact hours a week, above my required load. I also volunteered for extra classes so graduating students could finish that semester instead of waiting for the module to be offered again, and added tutorial hours for them.

A lesson in practice

Found, Chosen, Booked.

My 10-minute demonstration lesson for the MNU lecturer selection on 5 November 2026, where it scored highest. Built for Introduction to Marketing (MKT101) and Business Entrepreneurship (BUS301), it teaches segmentation, brand, the marketing mix and the sales process through one running case: Ruka Maldives, a twelve-room guesthouse on Dhiffushi that AI assistants left out when I tested them.

How the 10 minutes run
  1. Be the travellerStudents scan a QR code, pick a traveller and watch an AI search leave Ruka out.
  2. Four lessonsSegmentation and targeting, brand, the 7Ps and the seven-step sales process, each applied to Ruka.
  3. Think, pair, shareIf AI handles steps one to four, how does the manager’s role change?
  4. Fix it liveStudents apply four fixes and search again. Ruka moves up, except for divers.
  5. Practice and assessmentA tutorial with three AI assistants, and a group assignment.

    Research

    What the classroom made me ask.

    My first paper asked how Maldivian guesthouses could be seen online. My second mapped what it takes to run one. Today travellers increasingly ask AI tools where to stay, and small businesses don’t know whether they’re being recommended. That’s the question I want to study next.

    Research direction · intended doctoral focus

    How small tourism enterprises in the Maldives are found, and chosen, when AI tools make the recommendation.

    1. How visible are small Maldivian tourism businesses in AI-generated travel recommendations, compared with larger competitors?
    2. What stops them from adapting: skills, resources, or dependence on booking platforms?
    3. Can a simple diagnostic help owners see the gap and act on it?

    Working title: “From Friction to Flow”. [TODO: keep or change]

    Frameworks under consideration: technology-organisation-environment (TOE) and AI maturity, in a small-island context.
